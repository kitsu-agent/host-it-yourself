What do you need?

Good news first: you don't need expensive gear or a computer science degree to run your own photo cloud. A computer you (or someone you know) already owns, a cheap storage drive and an afternoon of patience are enough to start. Here's the honest checklist — space, hardware, cost and the skills it really takes — with no upselling and no surprises.

Storage: how much space your photos actually need

Start with the number everyone gets wrong. A modern phone photo is only 3–8 MB, so a typical camera roll of 2,000–5,000 photos takes up roughly 15–40 GB — less than you'd think. Video is the real weight: one minute of 4K footage is about 350 MB, so an hour fills around 21 GB. Add it up and most people need somewhere between 100 and 500 GB, which means a single affordable 1 TB drive holds several years of memories with room to spare. Immich also keeps smaller preview versions of every file, so leave a little headroom — but a 1 TB drive is genuinely plenty for a first setup.

Hardware: what can actually run Immich

The short answer: something you already own. An old laptop or desktop is the easiest start — it's free, it's x86, and it's built to run all day. Want low power and a small footprint? A Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with 4–8 GB of RAM (around €70–90) does the job, with one caveat: Immich's smart search leans on machine learning, which is heavy for a Pi, so keep expectations modest or add a small accelerator later. The sweet spot for most people is a mini PC (an Intel N100-class box with 8–16 GB, roughly €150–250): quiet, tiny, and sipping only 6–15 watts. If you already own a NAS with Docker, that works too. Whichever you pick, remember it runs 24/7 — a low-power box costs far less to keep on than an old gaming PC.

Cost: one-time, not a subscription

This is where self-hosting pays off. The Immich software itself is free and open source — €0, forever. The only real cost is storage: a 1 TB drive runs about €50–70, or you reuse a spare you already have. Hardware is €0 if you're reusing a laptop, around €70+ for a Pi, or €150+ for a mini PC. Compare that to renting: Google One is €1.99 per month for 100 GB and €9.99 per month for 2 TB — billed every month, for as long as you use it. A one-off €60 drive plus free software pays for itself in a couple of years, and after that you own the whole thing. No monthly fee, no price rises, no storage-full upsell.

Skills: honestly, what you need to know

Less than you fear. You follow a written guide and copy-paste commands — that's it, no programming. You'll want basic comfort with a terminal: typing a command, reading an error and searching for the answer, which is a skill you can learn in an afternoon. The first setup takes patience more than talent; after that, Immich updates itself and just runs. The documentation is clear, and the community is large and friendly, so when you hit a wall, someone has already solved it.
