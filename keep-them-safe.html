Keep them safe

Two things are true at once. Your photos are now safer than they've ever been — they're yours, on your hardware, not rented from a company that can change the rules. And your new server is one failed drive away from taking all of them with it, unless you plan for it. This is the part most beginner guides skip, so let's do it properly.

The rule that does all the work: 3-2-1

Almost everything you need to know fits in three numbers. Keep 3 copies of your photos, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 copy somewhere off-site. In practice that means: the originals on your server, a second copy on an external drive you plug in now and then, and a third copy that lives somewhere else entirely — a cloud service, or a friend's house. Any single failure — a dead disk, a spilled drink, a burglary, a house fire — can only ever take out one of the three. That's the whole idea, and it's the difference between "annoying" and "unrecoverable".

What "your photos" actually means in Immich

Here's the bit that trips people up. Immich stores your library in two separate places, and you need both: the photo and video files themselves (in your upload location), and the database, which holds all the metadata — which file is which, your albums, your faces, your edits. Back up only the files and you'll restore a messy pile of images with no albums and no names. Back up only the database and you'll restore an index pointing at photos that aren't there. The good news: Immich already backs up its database automatically — daily by default, keeping the last 14 — and you can change that in the admin settings. Just remember those database dumps hold metadata only, never the photos themselves.

Automate it, so it's not a chore

The only backups that survive are the ones you don't have to remember. Set up one scheduled job — a nightly script or a sync tool — that copies both the files and a fresh database dump to your external drive, and a second that pushes a copy off-site. Immich's own docs include a ready-made backup script you can run on a timer, so you're not writing anything from scratch. Set it once, check it once a month, and forget about it. The goal is a backup that happens whether or not you're thinking about it.

Test it — and know what a backup isn't

Two honest rules to close on. First, RAID is not a backup: mirroring two drives protects you from one disk dying, but not from deleting a folder, a power surge, or theft — it's one copy, not three. Second, a backup you've never restored isn't a backup yet — it's a hope. Once, on a quiet afternoon, practise restoring from your backup onto a spare machine. If your photos and albums come back, you're safe for real. If they don't, you found out now instead of on the worst day.
